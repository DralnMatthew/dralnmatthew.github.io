Abstract

Amortized Bayesian experimental design (BED) enables real-time design strategies by shifting acquisition cost offline, but existing methods remain tied to the prior and task distribution they were trained on and cannot exploit additional information available at deployment. We introduce in-context amortized BED with unified knowledge encoding (IMBUE), a BED framework that admits external knowledge at deployment through a unified in-context interface. IMBUE accepts two forms of such knowledge without retraining, namely user-specified prior knowledge and observations from previous instances of the experiment. Both are encoded as additional input tokens and processed in-context with the accumulated observations by a shared Transformer, and a learned reliability filter scores each external token against the observations and excludes inconsistent ones. Across four benchmarks, IMBUE accelerates early-stage information acquisition when the external knowledge is reliable, and remains close to the no-external baseline when it is not.

Method

Bayesian experimental design (BED) chooses each experiment to maximize the expected information gain (EIG) about the parameters of interest. Amortized BED trains a design policy offline, so at deployment it maps the observations gathered so far to the next design in a single forward pass. Existing policies, however, are trained under a fixed prior and have no input pathway for what an experimenter often knows about the specific instance: a user-specified prior over the parameters, or observations from previous instances of the same experiment. Such knowledge can also be unreliable, and conditioning on it without scrutiny can leave the policy worse off than a no-external baseline, a phenomenon known as negative transfer.

Prior tokens

A belief about each parameter θl is discretized into a normalized histogram over NB bins, which accepts multimodal, skewed or truncated beliefs without committing to a parametric family. An MLP maps the histogram to a token, and a learnable parameter-specific embedding, shared with that parameter's target token, ties the two together. Parameters without a user-specified prior get a uniform histogram.

Instance tokens

Each previous instance, a sequence of T design–outcome pairs, is compressed into a single token by a per-instance Transformer encoder shared across all K instances. The main encoder therefore receives K instance tokens instead of K·T raw observations, which would scale self-attention quadratically in K·T.

Reliability filter

A cross-attention block scores each in-context token against the embeddings of the experimental history, giving a score α ∈ [0, 1]. Tokens with α ≤ τ = 0.5 are excluded from self-attention through a key-padding mask. The scores are recomputed every round as observations accumulate; before the first observation arrives, all in-context tokens are excluded.

Training and loss

Training rollouts are simulated, so the ground-truth parameters are known during training and give oracle reliability labels for the filter; the labels are neither observed nor required at deployment. Previous instances are drawn from a 70/30 relevant/irrelevant mixture, and priors from an analogous mixture of calibrated, miscalibrated and uniform regimes, so that a single trained policy can absorb whatever information is supplied at deployment.

IMBUE adopts the shared Transformer backbone, head structure, per-step reward and variational training scheme of ALINE unchanged. A design head produces the policy, an inference head produces a Gaussian-mixture posterior, and both are trained jointly with the filter through L = λ1Lpolicy + λ2Linf + λ3Lfilter, with λ1 = λ2 = λ3 = 1 and an undiscounted reward, γ = 1. If every in-context token is excluded, the forward pass reduces to a history-only amortized BED policy.

Naive concatenation fails

Can an existing amortized policy use in-context knowledge supplied at deployment or at training time? On CES with K = 5 previous instances, ALINE*, which concatenates the instances only at deployment, falls below the original ALINE in both regimes: previous instances unseen during training disrupt the policy regardless of their reliability. ALINE-aux, retrained on the same 70/30 mixture as IMBUE, roughly matches ALINE under relevant input and trails it under irrelevant input, so retraining alone extracts little from reliable instances and only partly suppresses misleading information.

IMBUE exceeds ALINE under relevant input and stays close to it under irrelevant input.

Reliable in-context knowledge accelerates acquisition

On Location Finding, IMBUE receives one prior histogram per source coordinate. Under a strong prior, IMBUE is consistently higher than ALINE through the early and middle rollout, and reaches ALINE's final sPCE several steps earlier. The advantage narrows toward the end as the contribution of the prior diminishes relative to the accumulated observations. Under a miscalibrated prior, IMBUE stays close to the ALINE baseline through the rollout and finishes well above DAD, RL-BOED and the random reference.

The same acceleration holds on the previous-instance channel: on CES, K relevant previous instances raise IMBUE's sPCE above the K = 0 baseline, and the gain increases with K (table below).

IMBUE remains robust to unreliable in-context knowledge

On CES, IMBUE's sPCE increases with K when the previous instances are relevant. When they are irrelevant, IMBUE stays close to its K = 0 score at both values of K, while ALINE* degrades as K grows. DAD* and RL-BOED* drop under any added input regardless of its reliability. The same robustness appears on the prior channel under a miscalibrated prior (Location Finding, above).

Without retraining, IMBUE also does not degrade sharply under three deployment shifts absent from training: biased but partially relevant previous instances, a different noise model in the previous instances, and priors whose support excludes the true parameters (More results → Deployment shifts).

IMBUE handles mixed-relevance inputs per instance

So far the previous instances were either all relevant or all irrelevant. Here the K = 5 instances mix the two, testing whether IMBUE can retain relevant instance tokens and exclude irrelevant ones within a single forward pass. On 1D GP active learning, the policy chooses xt ∈ [−5, 5], observes a noisy evaluation of an unknown target function and is scored by the RMSE of its predictions on a held-out test set. Synthetic is a fresh draw from the GP prior used in training; Forrester, Higdon and Gramacy & Lee are analytic functions that probe out-of-distribution generalization. Relevant instances are per-instance perturbations of the target function, irrelevant ones unrelated 1D functions.

GP*, ALINE* and MT-GP degrade as the number of irrelevant instances grows, whereas IMBUE remains essentially flat on all four benchmarks. ALINE* shares IMBUE's backbone but concatenates the instances at deployment, so the backbone architecture alone does not provide this robustness. MT-GP, a multi-task GP that treats each previous instance as a separate task, fuses all K instances into a joint model with learned task-correlation weights; IMBUE instead makes a binary keep-or-discard decision per token, excluding tokens judged unreliable from self-attention.

Realistic psychometric task

The task infers the four parameters of a psychometric model from binary yes/no responses to stimulus intensities over T = 30 steps, scored on the threshold and slope, with K = 5 instances from previous participants. ALINE and the classical QUEST+ and Psi-marginal serve as no-external references, and without previous instances (K = 0) IMBUE is comparable to all three. With relevant previous instances, it attains lower joint RMSE than all of them throughout the rollout; its RMSE drops sharply in the first few steps, and the gap narrows toward the end. Its gains with relevant instances are larger at higher K.

With irrelevant previous instances, IMBUE tracks the no-external baselines closely, and its joint RMSE stays below that of IMBUE w/o filter, by a margin that grows and persists longer as K increases. ALINE*, by contrast, does not improve over its K = 0 RMSE even with relevant instances, and its RMSE grows with K under irrelevant ones (More results → Psychometric).

Ablations

Contributions of training, tokenization, and filtering

A staged ablation on CES with K = 5 starts from ALINE* and successively adds mixed-regime training (ALINE-aux), the instance-token interface (IMBUE w/o filter, retrained without the filter and keeping all instance tokens) and reliability filtering (IMBUE). Mixed-regime training and the instance-token interface improve mean sPCE over ALINE* under both reliable and unreliable inputs. Adding the filter further increases mean sPCE under biased and irrelevant inputs, while performance under relevant inputs remains similar. This is consistent with the filter's purpose: limiting negative transfer from unreliable instances and simply retaining reliable ones, for which no additional gain is expected. Under irrelevant inputs, the benefit of filtering is largest in the middle of CES rollouts, grows with K, and is more persistent on the psychometric task.

Retrieve-then-condition passes ALINE-aux only the two instances that best explain the observations collected so far; compared with ALINE-aux, it improves sPCE under irrelevant inputs but reduces it under relevant inputs, since its fixed top-two selection must discard useful instances. IMBUE's keep-or-discard decision has no such budget and can keep all five instances or drop all five at any round, depending on the observations accumulated so far. On the prior channel (Location Finding), however, the filter has little effect on sPCE across all four tested prior conditions, and IMBUE w/o filter retains the robustness to miscalibrated priors. Together with the CES and psychometric filter ablations, this indicates that filtering provides additional protection primarily against unreliable previous-instance input.

With and without the filter

With irrelevant previous instances on CES, the full IMBUE achieves higher sPCE than IMBUE w/o filter, with a clearer advantage when more irrelevant instances are supplied. The gap is most visible during the middle of the rollout and narrows as experimental observations accumulate. On the psychometric task the full IMBUE likewise keeps a lower joint RMSE, with a larger and more persistent gap when more irrelevant instances are supplied. With relevant instances, the two variants are indistinguishable on both tasks: when all instance tokens carry useful signal, removing the filter neither helps nor hurts.

What the filter learns

On Location Finding, the filter's scores for prior tokens settle close to 1 under a strong prior and stabilize around 0.8 under a weak prior. Under a miscalibrated prior they start at an intermediate level and decay toward 0 as observations accumulate, indicating decreasing trust in the provided prior; under a uniform prior they stay near 0 from the first rounds.

On CES, biased instances, a regime never seen in training, receive mean scores between those of relevant and irrelevant instances: the filter places the unseen regime between the two it was trained on rather than forcing it into either.

Binary versus soft gating

Soft gating scales each token's contribution to self-attention by its score α instead of keeping or excluding it at τ = 0.5. The two variants achieve similar sPCE with relevant and irrelevant previous instances, indicating no clear improvement from soft gating in these experiments.

Sensitivity to training mixture and filter loss weight

Increasing pgood, the probability that a prior marginal is calibrated during training, improves sPCE under strong priors but reduces it under miscalibrated priors, while weak and uniform priors are much less affected: the training mixture controls a trade-off between benefiting from informative priors and robustness to misleading ones, and the default pgood = 0.7 is a compromise within the tested range. The default λ3 = 1 and the smaller weight λ3 = 0.1 give nearly identical final sPCE under all four prior conditions, so the Location Finding results are insensitive to λ3 within the tested range.

Deployment shifts

The trained models are evaluated, without retraining, under three shifts that are not in the training mixture. IMBUE does not degrade sharply in any of them. These are three specific, plausible shifts and do not establish robustness to arbitrary deployment mismatch.

Biased but partially relevant instances (CES). All K = 5 instances are generated as in the relevant regime but share a systematic shift on a randomly selected subset of the parameters, so each is informative about the unshifted parameters and misleading about the shifted ones. IMBUE stays close to its K = 0 performance, while deployment-time concatenation degrades (Biased column of the staged ablation).

A different noise model in the previous instances (CES). Instance outcomes carry heavier-tailed Student-t noise at a larger scale instead of the Gaussian noise used in training. Relevant instances remain beneficial, and irrelevant ones leave performance close to K = 0.

Priors with incorrect support (Location Finding). The supplied prior assigns exactly zero probability to the bin containing the true parameter: a truncated prior concentrates its mass in a window away from it, and a boundary prior places its mass at the two ends of the support. There is no sharp degradation in sPCE under either.

Compute

On both CES and Location Finding, the full model keeps per-step inference time in the millisecond range. The reliability filter has 9,089 parameters, independent of K, the number of histogram bins and the task: 6.3% of the full CES model (145,408 parameters) and 7.3% of the full Location Finding model (123,744 parameters). At this scale the 2,000 candidate tokens dominate the sequence, so shortening the in-context segment from K·T = 50 concatenated observations to K = 5 instance tokens brings little latency benefit. The advantage of the token interface appears at larger K and T, since the in-context segment of ALINE* grows as K·T whereas that of IMBUE grows as K, so concatenation eventually exceeds the sequence-length budget.

On the GP benchmarks, IMBUE at K = 0 has inference time comparable to ALINE. With five relevant previous instances it remains substantially faster than the GP variants, which refit their models at each step, at a small absolute increase over ALINE for instance encoding and reliability filtering.

All experiments ran on NVIDIA H100 80GB GPUs, about 4,000 GPU hours in total across all stages. Training an IMBUE model for 105 epochs takes roughly 7 hours per benchmark.

Limitations

  • The in-context knowledge is structured: priors are histograms and previous instances are sequences of design–outcome pairs. Admitting richer forms, such as natural-language task descriptions, would bring amortized BED closer to the settings in which domain experts naturally encode their knowledge.
  • Priors enter as parameter-wise marginals, so parameter dependencies are not represented.
  • The likelihood, design and observation spaces are assumed to stay within the trained task family; the paper probes three specific deployment shifts.
  • The main qualitative conclusions hold across the tested candidate-pool sizes and constructions, but candidate enumeration does not scale to very high-dimensional designs; extending IMBUE to continuous design spaces through actor-critic formulations would broaden its applicability.
  • The filter is less reliable in the first few rounds, when the limited experimental history makes it difficult to distinguish informative from misleading external inputs.
