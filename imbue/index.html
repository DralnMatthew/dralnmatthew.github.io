IMBUE: Amortized Bayesian Experimental Design with In-Context Knowledge Conditioning

Use priors and past experiments at deployment without retraining, while staying robust to unreliable inputs.

The challenge: using knowledge at deployment

A new experiment rarely starts from zero. An expert may have a useful prior, or previous participants may already provide relevant observations. How can a trained policy use that knowledge to choose its next measurement?

Existing amortized Bayesian experimental design policies are tied to their training prior, with no input pathway for this extra knowledge at deployment. Simply adding previous observations can also hurt when they come from a different population. IMBUE accepts both priors and previous experiments in context, without retraining, and checks their reliability as new observations arrive.

Highlights

  1. Naive concatenation is not enough. Feeding extra inputs to existing amortized BED policies can lower performance, even when the inputs are informative. CES results

  2. One interface for priors and past experiments. Both enter a single trained policy as tokens at deployment, without retraining; a learned filter checks each token against the observations. Method

  3. Faster when reliable, robust when not. Across four benchmarks, IMBUE accelerates early information acquisition with reliable knowledge and stays close to the no-external baseline when the knowledge is unreliable. All results

Read the paper abstract

Amortized Bayesian experimental design (BED) enables real-time design strategies by shifting acquisition cost offline, but existing methods remain tied to the prior and task distribution they were trained on and cannot exploit additional information available at deployment. We introduce in-context amortized BED with unified knowledge encoding (IMBUE), a BED framework that admits external knowledge at deployment through a unified in-context interface. IMBUE accepts two forms of such knowledge without retraining, namely user-specified prior knowledge and observations from previous instances of the experiment. Both are encoded as additional input tokens and processed in-context with the accumulated observations by a shared Transformer, and a learned reliability filter scores each external token against the observations and excludes inconsistent ones. Across four benchmarks, IMBUE accelerates early-stage information acquisition when the external knowledge is reliable, and remains close to the no-external baseline when it is not.

Method: knowledge as in-context

IMBUE builds on the shared Transformer and design–inference heads of ALINE. Its new interface represents two kinds of external knowledge as tokens. A learned filter compares each token with the current experimental history before the policy selects the next design.

Problem setup

Let \(\theta \in \Theta \subseteq \mathbb{R}^L\) be the latent parameters of the system under study. A design \(x\) returns an outcome \(y \sim p(y \mid \theta, x)\). Over \(T\) rounds, starting from an empty history \(\mathcal{D}_0 = \emptyset\), a design policy \(\pi_\psi\) proposes \(x_t\), the experiment returns \(y_t\), and the history grows to \(\mathcal{D}_t = \mathcal{D}_{t-1} \cup \{(x_t, y_t)\}\). The inference target \(Z\) is either the parameters, \(Z = \theta\), or outcomes \(Z = y^\star\) at test inputs \(x^\star\).

IMBUE adds in-context knowledge available at deployment, \(C = (C_{\text{prior}}, C_{\text{obs}})\), and the policy proposes \(x_t \sim \pi_\psi(\cdot \mid \mathcal{D}_{t-1}, C)\):

  • Prior channel \(C_{\text{prior}}\): a user's marginal beliefs \(\rho_l(\theta_l)\) over the individual parameters \(\theta_1, \ldots, \theta_L\).
  • Previous-instance channel \(C_{\text{obs}} = \{\mathcal{A}_k\}_{k=1}^{K}\): \(K\) earlier runs of the same experiment, each with \(T\) design–outcome pairs \(\mathcal{A}_k = \{(x_i^{(k)}, y_i^{(k)})\}_{i=1}^{T}\).

Either channel may be empty, which recovers standard amortized BED. The policy is judged by the information the experiment gathers about \(Z\), given \(C\):

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathcal{U}(\psi; C) &= I_{p_\psi}(Z; \mathcal{D}_T \mid C) \\ &= H(Z \mid C) - H_{p_\psi}(Z \mid \mathcal{D}_T, C). \end{aligned} \]

Training maximizes its expectation over a distribution \(p_{\text{train}}(C)\) that mixes reliable and unreliable knowledge.

In-context token interface

IMBUE represents each piece of in-context knowledge as a token, so a single trained Transformer accepts either channel at deployment without retraining. Priors and previous observations differ in form, length and meaning, so each channel has its own token type.

Prior tokens

Each marginal belief \(\rho_l\) is discretized into a normalized histogram \(\boldsymbol{\rho}_l = (\rho_l^{(1)}, \ldots, \rho_l^{(N_B)})\) over \(N_B\) uniform bins \(B_1, \ldots, B_{N_B}\) covering the support of \(\theta_l\):

\[ \rho_l^{(b)} = \frac{\int_{B_b} \rho_l(u)\,du}{\sum_{b'=1}^{N_B} \int_{B_{b'}} \rho_l(u)\,du}. \]

This non-parametric form accepts multimodal, skewed or truncated beliefs without committing to a parametric family. An MLP \(f_{\text{prior}}\) maps the histogram to the model dimension \(D\), and a learnable parameter-specific embedding \(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_l\) ties the token to its parameter:

\[ \mathbf{e}_l^{\text{prior}} = f_{\text{prior}}(\boldsymbol{\rho}_l) + \boldsymbol{\zeta}_l \in \mathbb{R}^D. \]

The same \(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_l\) serves as the target token for \(\theta_l\) in the backbone, which aligns each prior with the parameter it describes. Parameters without a user-specified prior get a uniform histogram.

Instance tokens

Feeding all \(K \cdot T\) raw design–outcome pairs into the main encoder would make self-attention scale quadratically in \(K \cdot T\) and tie the encoder's input length to the previous instances' horizon. Instead, a per-instance encoder \(g_{\text{aux}}\), a separate Transformer shared across all \(K\) instances, compresses each instance into a single token:

\[ \mathbf{r}_k = g_{\text{aux}}(\mathcal{A}_k) = \frac{1}{n_s} \sum_{s=1}^{n_s} \mathbf{h}_s^{(k)} \in \mathbb{R}^D. \]

Inside \(g_{\text{aux}}\), \(n_s\) learnable summary slots are prepended to the embedded pairs of instance \(k\) and attend to them through self-attention; \(\mathbf{h}_s^{(k)}\) is the output at slot \(s\). Averaging over the slots gives the main encoder one token per instance.

Joining the backbone

Prior and instance tokens are the in-context tokens. Those kept by the reliability filter join ALINE's three token groups in the shared encoder: context tokens for the history \(\mathcal{D}_t\), query tokens for the candidate designs, and target tokens, one per parameter when \(Z = \theta\) or one per test input when \(Z = y^\star\). A structured attention mask lets every token attend to the in-context and context tokens, and lets query tokens also attend to the target tokens. The design head reads the query positions to give \(\pi_\psi\), and the inference head reads the target positions to give \(q_\phi\), a Gaussian mixture over the components of \(Z\).

Reliability filter

External knowledge is not always reliable: a prior may concentrate its mass in the wrong region of parameter space, and a previous instance may come from a different population. Conditioning on such tokens without scrutiny can pull the policy away from what the observations support. The filter \(f_\omega\) therefore scores every in-context token \(\mathbf{e}_j\) against the embeddings \(\mathbf{E}_t\) of the history \(\mathcal{D}_t\):

\[ \alpha_j^{(t)} = \sigma\bigl(f_\omega(\mathbf{e}_j; \mathbf{E}_t)\bigr) \in [0, 1], \]

where \(f_\omega\) is a cross-attention block that uses the token as the query and the history as keys and values, followed by a small scoring MLP, and \(\sigma\) is the logistic sigmoid.

  • Keep or discard. Tokens with \(\alpha_j^{(t)} \le \tau = 0.5\) are excluded from self-attention in the backbone through a key-padding mask.
  • Re-scored every round. Reliability is judged by consistency with the observations, so the same token can receive different scores as \(\mathcal{D}_t\) grows. Before the first observation there is nothing to compare against, and all in-context tokens are excluded.
  • Falls back to the history. When every in-context token is excluded, the forward pass reduces to a history-only amortized BED policy.

Training

IMBUE keeps ALINE's backbone, output heads, per-step reward and variational training scheme unchanged. What is new is trained alongside them: the token interface, on a mixture of reliable and unreliable inputs, and the reliability filter, supervised with labels computed from the simulated ground truth.

Simulated rollouts with reliable and unreliable knowledge

Each training rollout draws a ground-truth parameter \(\theta^\star\) and in-context knowledge \(C\) for the task's channel, deliberately including knowledge that is wrong:

  • Previous instances. A random number \(K\) of instances is drawn. Each is relevant with probability 0.7, generated from \(\theta^{(k)} = \theta^\star + \eta_k\) with a small Gaussian perturbation \(\eta_k\), or irrelevant with probability 0.3, generated from a \(\theta^{(k)}\) drawn independently of \(\theta^\star\). Its designs are drawn uniformly and its outcomes simulated from \(p(y \mid \theta^{(k)}, x)\).
  • Priors. The prior is uniform (probability 0.2) or Gaussian with a random location and width. Each parameter is then calibrated, with \(\theta_l^\star\) drawn from its histogram, or miscalibrated, with that draw shifted toward the far side of the support.

The policy then runs a \(T\)-round rollout, \(x_t \sim \pi_\psi(\cdot \mid \mathcal{D}_{t-1}, C)\) and \(y_t \sim p(y \mid \theta^\star, x_t)\). After every round the inference head evaluates \(\log q_\phi^{(t)} = \log q_\phi(Z^\star \mid \mathcal{D}_t, C)\) at the realized target \(Z^\star\), and the filter re-scores the in-context tokens.

Reliability labels from the simulator

Because \(\theta^\star\) is known in simulation, every in-context token gets an oracle reliability label. A prior token is scored by how much more mass its histogram puts on the bin \(b^\star\) containing \(\theta_l^\star\) than a uniform histogram would,

\[ r_j^{\text{prior}} = \sigma\!\left(\beta \log \frac{\rho_l^{(b^\star)}}{1/N_B}\right), \]

and an instance token by how close its generating parameters \(\theta^{(k)}\) are to \(\theta^\star\),

\[ r_j^{\text{inst}} = \exp\!\left(-\kappa \sum_{l=1}^{L} \left(\frac{\theta_l^{(k)} - \theta_l^\star}{r_l^{\max} - r_l^{\min}}\right)^{2}\right), \]

where \([r_l^{\min}, r_l^{\max}]\) is the support of \(\theta_l\) (or a fixed range if it is unbounded) and \(\beta, \kappa > 0\) set the sharpness. Thresholding at \(1/2\) gives binary targets \(c_j = \mathbb{I}[r_j > 1/2]\). On GP active learning, where the target is \(y^\star\), the label is binary and tied to the regime that generated the instance. The labels use only quantities the simulator already draws; they are neither observed nor needed at deployment.

A lower bound on information gain

The posterior inside \(\mathcal{U}\) is intractable for general likelihoods, so IMBUE, like ALINE, trains against the Barber–Agakov variational lower bound, with the inference head \(q_\phi\) as the variational distribution:

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathcal{U}(\psi; C) \;\ge\;& H(Z \mid C) \\ &+ \mathbb{E}_{p_\psi(Z, \mathcal{D}_T \mid C)}\bigl[\log q_\phi(Z \mid \mathcal{D}_T, C)\bigr]. \end{aligned} \]

\(H(Z \mid C)\) depends on neither network, so training maximizes, jointly in the policy \(\psi\) and the inference head \(\phi\), the expected log-density that \(q_\phi\) assigns to the true target at the end of the rollout.

Three losses

Averaged over a minibatch of rollouts, these quantities enter three losses, combined with equal weights \(\lambda_1 = \lambda_2 = \lambda_3 = 1\):

\[ \mathcal{L}(\psi, \phi, \omega) = \lambda_1 \mathcal{L}_{\text{policy}} + \lambda_2 \mathcal{L}_{\text{inf}} + \lambda_3 \mathcal{L}_{\text{filter}}. \]

Inference loss. The inference head learns to infer the realized target from the history and the retained tokens at every round:

\[ \mathcal{L}_{\text{inf}} = -\frac{1}{T+1} \sum_{t=0}^{T} \log q_\phi^{(t)}. \]

Its final-round term optimizes the bound above with respect to \(\phi\) and, for a fixed policy, tightens it; the earlier rounds keep \(q_\phi\) accurate enough to reward the policy.

Policy loss. The design head is trained by policy gradient with ALINE's dense per-step reward \(R_t = \log q_\phi^{(t)} - \log q_\phi^{(t-1)}\), the gain in log-density after each new observation:

\[ \mathcal{L}_{\text{policy}} = -\frac{1}{T} \sum_{t=1}^{T} \gamma^{t} R_t \log \pi_\psi(x_t \mid \mathcal{D}_{t-1}, C), \]

with \(\gamma = 1\). Gradients stop at \(q_\phi\), so the policy cannot raise its reward by changing the inference head. The rewards telescope to the final log-density minus a term the policy cannot affect, which favors designs that raise the bound.

Filter loss. The filter is trained as a classifier against the oracle labels, with binary cross-entropy over all rounds and all \(|C|\) in-context tokens:

\[ \mathcal{L}_{\text{filter}} = \frac{1}{T\,|C|} \sum_{t=1}^{T} \sum_{j=1}^{|C|} \mathrm{BCE}\bigl(\alpha_j^{(t)}, c_j\bigr). \]

It is zero when \(C\) is empty, is not derived from the bound, and is detached from the embedders shared with the rest of the network.

Two-stage schedule

The reward \(R_t\) is informative only once \(q_\phi\) is accurate. For the first \(E_{\text{warm}}\) epochs, designs are therefore drawn uniformly at random from the candidate pool and only \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{inf}}\) and \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{filter}}\) are optimized. Afterwards the policy selects the designs and all three losses are optimized jointly, with AdamW and a cosine learning-rate schedule.

At deployment no labels, gradient steps or retraining are needed: a user supplies a prior, previous instances, or nothing, and each design takes one forward pass in which the filter decides, from the observations alone, which tokens to keep. The paper gives the full algorithm, architecture and hyperparameters.

Experiments and results

Four benchmarks test useful priors, previous experiments, and mixtures of relevant and irrelevant inputs. Across these settings, reliable knowledge accelerates acquisition, while unreliable knowledge leaves IMBUE close to its no-external-input baseline.

01 / Location finding

Expert priors accelerate acquisition

The task is to locate two hidden sources from 20 noisy measurements. IMBUE receives a prior histogram for each source coordinate, supplied at deployment.

With a strong prior, it reaches ALINE's final information level several measurements earlier. With a prior shifted away from the true locations, it stays close to the ALINE baseline. The same trained policy handles both cases.

02 / Preference elicitation (CES)

Past experiments help when they are relevant

CES (Constant Elasticity of Substitution) infers a person's preferences from sequential choices. Data from previous respondents can be useful context, but only when their preferences are related. With five relevant instances, IMBUE's sPCE rises from 13.46 to 13.93; with five irrelevant ones, it remains close to its no-input score at 13.14.

Simply concatenating these observations into existing policies is less reliable. ALINE*'s score falls to 10.16 with five irrelevant instances, while DAD and RL-BOED degrade even with relevant ones. An asterisk marks this concatenation baseline.

03 / GP active learning

Useful and misleading inputs can arrive together

Here the policy learns an unknown function by choosing where to observe it next. Five previous instances mix related and unrelated functions. As more instances become irrelevant, GP*, ALINE*, and multi-task GP baselines lose accuracy; IMBUE's prediction error stays essentially flat on all four benchmarks.

Synthetic functions test the training distribution, while Forrester, Higdon, and Gramacy & Lee probe generalization beyond it.

04 / Psychometric model

Transfer knowledge between participants

A psychometric experiment estimates a participant's response threshold and slope from binary responses to stimulus intensities. IMBUE can use five previous participants as context for the new experiment.

Relevant participants reduce estimation error throughout the 30-measurement experiment, especially early on. With irrelevant participants, IMBUE tracks the no-external-input references ALINE, QUEST+, and Psi-marginal closely.

Where the gains come from

The token interface and training on both reliable and unreliable inputs account for most of the gains and robustness. The filter provides additional protection against conflicting previous-instance inputs, particularly on CES and the psychometric task. On Location Finding, removing the filter has little effect on the tested prior conditions. Read the full ablations in the paper.

The paper and its appendix include further comparisons, deployment shifts, sensitivity studies, and computational costs.

Scope and limitations

IMBUE accepts structured inputs within the trained task family: prior histograms and sequences of previous observations. Priors describe individual parameters, without modeling dependencies between them. The filter is less reliable in the earliest rounds, when little evidence is available. Richer knowledge formats and continuous design policies that avoid enumerating large candidate pools remain directions for future work.
